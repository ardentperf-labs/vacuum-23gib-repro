# Write resumption diagnosis (final invalid-pilot diagnosis)

This was an invalid, user-stopped pilot: inherited preparation removed archive references, so the intended archive configuration was absent. The >2-hour objective was not evaluated. Within the captured treatment interval, its roughly 36-minute write stall is strongly consistent with synchronous replication waiting for its only eligible standby. It is not evidence of an unexplained client hang: the primary required one of hdd-pg-2 (synchronous_standby_names = ANY 1 ("hdd-pg-2")) and synchronous_commit was on. The sole sender disappeared after the replica walreceiver was terminated, returned first as catchup/potential, and was first captured as streaming/quorum at 02:33:38.207 UTC. The client fixture’s progress counter rose about 0.52 seconds later. Native pgbench logs now show the exact transaction-completion gap and place the first resumed completion inside the sampled quorum-transition bracket.

The SIGTERM occurred at 01:56:51.569 UTC, 73.729 seconds after the scheduled +600-second point because the runner’s first `pg_terminate_backend` attempt returned false and sent no signal. The primary had no replication sender from 01:56:52.089 until 02:24:02.040. On reconnect the sender was `catchup/potential`; it did not become `streaming/quorum` until 02:33:38.207. The fixture’s `sum(update_count)` stayed at 8,530 from 01:56:52.427 through 02:33:37.294. There were 1,986 successful client observations, no sample errors, and the same ten writer PIDs were present in each; all ten were observed on `SyncRep` in 1,979 samples. The largest adjacent sample gap was 5.844 seconds, so the point samples do not establish continuous wait state between samples. The first progress observation after the plateau was 9,539 at 02:33:38.728.

The control retained a streaming/quorum sender and kept advancing: its fixture counter increased from 8,529 at 01:56:52 to 16,693 at 02:23:57 and 19,497 at 02:33:37. This control behavior is consistent with the treatment’s synchronous-replication explanation. No primary/replica role transition or primary setting variant was observed in the sampled coverage; the live Cluster capture still named `hdd-pg-1` as primary.

At the quorum transition, the sender’s flush LSN and replica’s flushed LSN were `57/24613A0`, about 952 bytes behind the primary flush LSN `57/2461758`. The replica replay LSN was still `51/4FC69438`, roughly 22.8 GiB behind. This is expected for `synchronous_commit=on`: acknowledgement waits for remote WAL flush, not replay/application. Therefore the large replay backlog does not contradict the writes becoming eligible to commit once the standby was counted in quorum.

The verified `VACUUM_COMPLETE` marker records treatment completion at 02:32:23.150343 UTC and control completion at 02:47:54.220460 UTC. The treatment sender entered quorum 75.057 seconds after VACUUM completion. This timing is consistent with a catch-up threshold being crossed just after VACUUM ended; the capture does not establish that VACUUM completion itself caused release. Marker contents and the read-only capture command output are preserved in `vacuum-completion-marker-readonly-capture.json`. The run demonstrated about 36m45s of no fixture progress, not the hoped-for >2 hours.

## Was WAL being restored from object storage?

The capture does not support an object-storage fetch or error. PostgreSQL had a generic CNPG `manager wal-restore` command configured, and replica startup produced 58 intermittent `RestoreCommand` wait samples while the receiver was absent. However, the live Cluster had no backup, `externalClusters`, or plugins; there were no Backup or ScheduledBackup objects, and the Barman ObjectStore CRD was not installed. Preparation metadata says the matched local snapshot pair did not require an external WAL archive and its archive references had been removed. No object-store fetch success or error appears in the captured logs. The only captured wal-restore log says it refused future history file `00000003.history` because timeline 3 was later than the cluster’s timeline 2. The installed CNPG v1.30.1 no-backup wal-restore path reports no configured backup rather than fetching an external object; the no-backup archiver path can return success without uploading WAL, so `pg_stat_archiver` counters are not proof of object-storage uploads ([wal-restore source](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/blob/v1.30.1/internal/cmd/manager/walrestore/cmd.go), [archiver source](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/blob/v1.30.1/pkg/management/postgres/archiver/archiver.go)).

Thus, the evidence shows that the recovery process entered the generic restore-command wait category, but not that it contacted object storage. The remaining WAL source/path behavior is not fully observable from this capture. The specific “object-storage restore error” theory is unsubstantiated; the stronger explanation for write release is the sender’s transition into synchronous quorum.

## Native client completion evidence

Both clients were stopped gracefully at 03:43:46 UTC after the user halted this invalid pilot. I parsed every final seven-field native pgbench log row and verified the totals against both client summaries and server-side committed-update counters. Treatment: 40,627 raw valid completion rows, 40,627 logged/client/server commits, 0 failed, exact reconciliation. Control: 40,622 rows and commits, 0 failed, exact reconciliation. The common 01:56:00–02:35:00 UTC window contains 11,668 treatment rows and 11,663 control rows. The earlier point-in-time captures taken while pgbench was active had truncated lines; the final logs are clean and the earlier control partial record is now fully resolved. Per-file hashes and reconciliation facts are in `write-resumption-diagnosis.json`.

Across the final treatment logs, the longest adjacent completion gap is 2,206.030588 seconds, from 01:56:51.444367 to 02:33:37.474955 UTC. The first post-gap transaction has 2,205.847976 seconds of execution time (latency minus schedule_lag). That is one transaction completing after nearly the full gap; it does not establish that each writer statement occupied the entire interval. The native first post-gap completion at 02:33:37.474955 falls inside the approximately one-second sample bracket for the sender’s transition to quorum: the preceding PostgreSQL query sample at 02:33:37.024843 reported catchup/potential, and the next at 02:33:38.024760 reported streaming/quorum. Therefore exact ordering against the actual transition is unknown. The fixture counter’s first sampled increase at 02:33:38.728117 is 0.521117 seconds after the first collector-captured quorum row.

Across the final control logs, the maximum closed completion gap is 2.985099 seconds (the 2.840603-second gap above was only the common diagnostic-window maximum), and maximum transaction execution was 2.740395 seconds.

The first post-stall ten-second treatment bucket contains 10,565 completions (1,056.5 completions/s). The committed-update counter increased from 8,530 at 02:33:37.294 to 19,159 at 02:33:47.790. This is the treatment clients draining delayed transactions scheduled during the outage, not a new sustainable rate: within the bucket median schedule lag was 1,131.937 seconds and median execution time was 8.851 milliseconds. pgbench documents that with `--rate`, the expected schedule is based on the initial client start time and delayed transactions can catch up ([pgbench rate scheduling](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/pgbench.html#PGBENCH-RATE-LIMIT)). The native completion graph correctly shows completions by recorded completion timestamp, but this post-stall spike should not be read as the configured 5 TPS offered rate or as sustained throughput.

## Pilot validity and caveats

The user stopped both lanes because preparation lacked the required archive configuration and the replica had an estimated 72 minutes of replay remaining. Both VACUUMs returned exit code 0, and both clients drained gracefully with native logs fully reconciled. However, both observation windows ended around 7,091 seconds after VACUUM start, below the 8,400-second minimum. Replay catch-up and final healthy post-recovery observation were not completed. The >2-hour objective was not evaluated. This invalid incomplete pilot is not a negative outcome from a completed two-hour test. At observation end, treatment still recorded 9,329,301,776 bytes of replica replay lag.

This was an OS SIGTERM to the replica walreceiver, not a naturally elapsed wal_sender_timeout; the signal was late by 73.729 seconds. The progress measure is the server-side client-fixture sum(update_count), not PostgreSQL xact_commit. Point samples show the same ten treatment writer PIDs present and all ten on SyncRep in most samples, but sampler gaps prevent a claim that the wait state was continuously observed. Archive references were deliberately removed by the inherited vacuum-only preparation; this run was not configured to test an external object-store WAL restore path.

## Evidence

For the PostgreSQL mechanism, version 16 documentation says `synchronous_commit=on` waits for remote WAL flush, while `remote_apply` waits for replay; the PostgreSQL 16 source releases synchronous waiters only from streaming/stopping senders, not a sender still in catch-up ([WAL setting](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/runtime-config-wal.html#GUC-SYNCHRONOUS-COMMIT), [synchronous replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/warm-standby.html#SYNCHRONOUS-REPLICATION), [waiter release code](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/blob/REL_16_STABLE/src/backend/replication/syncrep.c), [walsender catch-up code](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/blob/REL_16_STABLE/src/backend/replication/walsender.c)).

The structured evidence, point-in-time hashes, final native-log manifests, and source-file list are in `write-resumption-diagnosis.json`. The supporting raw or already-redacted evidence is under `results/measurement/`, `results/instrumentation/`, and `results/diagnosis/public/`; secret-bearing raw command/config captures were not copied into this summary.
